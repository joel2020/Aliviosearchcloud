Most small business owners think growth means more leads. It usually doesn't. The fastest revenue most SMBs can find is already inside the business — quoted leads that went silent, dormant customers, missed calls from last quarter, ghosted proposals.

This is the 90-day plan we use with Alivio customers to surface that revenue. It assumes a real working business, not a startup. The goal is recovered cash in the bank by day 90, not a beautiful slide deck.

## Where lost revenue actually hides

Before you can recover it, you have to know where it lives. In most SMBs, lost revenue clusters in five places:

1. **Quotes that never got a follow-up.**
2. **Missed calls from the past 6–12 months.**
3. **Old customers who used you once and never came back.**
4. **Proposals that were "almost there" but ghosted.**
5. **Form fills and inquiries from cold sources you never replied to.**

You don't need to fix all five at once. The 90-day plan picks the top two for your business and turns them into recovered revenue first.

## Days 1–14: Audit and centralize

You can't recover what you can't see. The first two weeks are about visibility.

- **Pull every quoted lead from the past 12 months** into a single sheet — one row per lead, columns for stage, status, last contact, ticket size.
- **Pull missed call logs** from your phone system or VoIP provider.
- **Export your CRM or spreadsheet contacts.** Tag everyone who hasn't been contacted in 6 months as "dormant."
- **Run a free [Revenue Audit](/blog/why-small-businesses-lose-leads)** so you have a number — what those leaks are roughly worth in your business.

By day 14 you should know two things: which leak is biggest, and what fixing it is worth.

## Days 15–30: Plug the biggest leak

Pick the single biggest leak and fix it first. For most SMBs that's either missed calls or quoted-lead silence.

- **If it's missed calls:** Deploy the Missed Call Agent and start texting back every missed call within 30 seconds. Expect to see meetings booked in week 1.
- **If it's quoted-lead silence:** Deploy the Follow-Up Agent on every quote that hasn't replied in the past 30 days. Run a 5-touch cadence over 14 days.

Don't try to fix everything at once. The point is to bank a measurable win in the first 30 days so the rest of the program has momentum.

## Days 31–60: Run the reactivation play

This is where the biggest dollar amounts usually come from. The reactivation play has four steps:

1. **Segment the dormant list** by source, last activity, and ticket size.
2. **Pick the segment most likely to re-engage** — usually customers from 6–18 months ago who had a good experience.
3. **Run a soft, useful 4-touch sequence.** Not "buy now." Something more like, "hey, just checking in — wanted to share a couple of things we've added since we last spoke."
4. **Route every reply to a human within minutes.**

The Reactivation Agent runs this cadence in the background. A typical SMB with 1,000 dormant contacts sees 20–50 real conversations restart and 5–15 deals close in the first 30 days of the play.

## Days 61–80: Rebuild the proposal pipeline

The "almost won" pile is the most under-managed asset in most SMBs. These are prospects who liked you, talked to you, got a proposal, and then went quiet — usually for reasons that have nothing to do with you.

- **Pull every proposal from the past 12 months that didn't close.**
- **Tag each one** by reason: timing, budget, competitor, ghosted.
- **Run a custom touch for the "ghosted" segment** with a low-friction ask — "any chance the timing is better now?"
- **For the "competitor" segment**, send one honest "how's it going?" message. A meaningful number of those deals come back when the competitor disappoints.

This is unglamorous work. It's also some of the highest-margin revenue in the building because you've already done the selling.

## Days 81–90: Lock in the operating rhythm

By day 80, you should have:

- The biggest leak plugged with an always-on agent.
- A measurable amount of revenue recovered from reactivation.
- A re-engaged proposal pipeline.

The last 10 days are about making this the new normal:

- **Set weekly review cadences** — what was captured, what was followed up, what closed.
- **Define quiet hours and message caps** so the engine stays customer-friendly.
- **Document the playbooks** so when you hire later, the new person inherits a working system.
- **Plan the next leak** to plug — usually instant response or outbound.

By day 90, recovered revenue should be flowing in. Most Alivio customers see between $30k and $250k of recovered revenue in the first 90 days, depending on business size and which leaks they had.

## What this isn't

This plan doesn't:

- Magically generate new pipeline out of thin air. It just stops the existing pipeline from leaking.
- Replace your sales judgment. It surfaces opportunities; you close them.
- Work without clean data. The first two weeks of audit and centralization are not optional.
